# Scoping a draft in two words at a time

Answering a run of clarifying questions while pruning a draft summary. The far end of Kyle's
terseness range — no example elsewhere in this skill goes this short and still counts as a real
decision.

---

> Q: What's the top focus today, given a midday meeting eats a chunk of the day?
> A: **"PLAT-217 to the finish"**
>
> Q: Do you want the two stale PRs on today's list?
> A: **"Leave them off"**
>
> Q: Should the speculative CLI proposal also show up on today's list?
> A: **"Drop it"**
> Q (follow-up): Drop it from today only, or off the summary entirely?
> A: **"Off entirely"**
>
> Q: You merged one PR, and fixed a break in CI while landing it. How do you want these listed?
> A: **"Fold into one bullet"**
>
> Q: You spent real time pushing back on a risky rename — include it?
> A: **"Skip it"**

---

## What to copy

- **Two words can still be a real decision.** "Leave them off," "Drop it," "Skip it" — not
  non-answers, complete answers.
- **Exclusion is stated, not left to silence.** Leaving something off is the answer itself, not an
  unanswered question sitting in the draft.
- **A clarifying follow-up gets resolved in kind, not re-explained from scratch.** "Off entirely"
  — two more words, not a paragraph walking back through the original ask.
- **Two pieces of related work compress into one line.** "Fold into one bullet" beats one bullet
  per artifact, even when the artifacts are technically separate.
- **Real friction doesn't automatically make the cut.** Doing the work and reporting the work are
  different decisions — "Skip it" is a legitimate answer to the second even when the first
  happened.
