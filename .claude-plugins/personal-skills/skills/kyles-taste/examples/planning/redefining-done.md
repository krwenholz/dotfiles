# Redefining the bar mid-review

An agent had produced an overly strict security review. Rather than accept or reject it wholesale,
Kyle restates what "done" actually means for this piece of work before asking for a second pass.

---

> I should help with a little bit of a reframing here. The goal is not perfect security
> protection. What we're really shooting for is best effort folks not stomping on each other's
> feet. These are running in fairly well-trusted environments and against an account that's okay
> to blow away. In that light, would you reconsider any of your points?

---

## What to copy

- **Names the actual bar in one sentence before evaluating anything against it.** "The goal is not
  X. What we're really shooting for is Y." — not a rebuttal of individual findings, a reset of the
  standard they're being measured against.
- **States the risk tolerance as the reason, not an excuse.** Trusted environment, disposable
  resource — the lowered bar has a concrete justification, not just "don't worry about it."
- **Invites the other side to revise its own conclusions.** "Would you reconsider any of your
  points?" — not "ignore points 2 and 4," a request to re-run the judgment with the corrected
  frame.
